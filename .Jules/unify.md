## 2025-03-08 - Reordering Sections for Narrative Flow
**Learning:** Selling the benefits before asking for the commitment (the steps) works better. Users need to understand *why* they should care (the "What You Gain" section) before they are willing to engage with the *how* (the "The Path Forward" section). Placing steps right before the final CTA creates a smoother transition into taking action.
**Action:** When evaluating landing page flow, always ensure the value proposition and benefits are presented before outlining the process or asking the user to commit to a series of steps.

## 2024-05-24 - Value Proposition Before Process
**Learning:** Landing pages that present the process or philosophy before the benefits often fail to answer "why this matters" soon enough, leading to weaker persuasive flow. The "half our waking hours" statistic was also repeated in the hero and problem sections, causing redundancy.
**Action:** Reorder sections to present benefits (What You Gain) before the process/philosophy (The Philosophy). Rewrite the hero copy to avoid stepping on the main statistic of the next section, building a more coherent top-to-bottom narrative.

## 2024-05-24 - Reassuring Quotes as Bridges
**Learning:** Quotes that reassure the user (e.g., "you don't have to give up the internet") belong immediately before the philosophy or process that explains how, not right after presenting the problem.
**Action:** When evaluating landing page flow, use quotes as narrative bridges to transition from the problem/benefits into the "how it works" philosophy.
