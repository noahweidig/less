Lifestyle

The things you own end up owning you.

The Weight of Things

Every object in your home asks something of you — attention, maintenance, money, and mental space. The average American home has doubled in size since 1970, and we still can't fit everything.

Physical Clutter = Mental Clutter

Research shows that visual clutter elevates cortisol levels. A cluttered home is a stressful home — not because of the mess, but because of the unfinished decisions each object represents.

The Maintenance Tax

Every possession has a cost beyond its price tag: cleaning, repairing, organizing, insuring, and eventually disposing of it. Own less and that tax disappears entirely.

The 80/20 of Your Wardrobe

The average person wears 20% of their clothes 80% of the time. The rest is inventory — clothing you store, clean, and move without ever wearing.

"The ability to simplify means to eliminate the unnecessary so that the necessary may speak."
— Hans Hofmann

Core Practices

You don't need to live out of a backpack. Start with three principles that will quietly transform your relationship with stuff.

The 90-Day Rule

If you haven't used something in 90 days and can't imagine a specific reason you'll use it in the next 90, it earns no place in your home. Apply this ruthlessly to clothes, kitchen gadgets, and hobby gear.

One In, One Out

Every new thing that enters your home must displace something old. This single constraint prevents accumulation from resuming by default and makes every purchase a deliberate trade.

The Capsule Wardrobe

Build a small collection of high-quality, versatile pieces that all work together. Fewer decisions each morning. Less laundry. More clarity. The goal isn't 33 items — it's intentionality about what you keep.

The Declutter Protocol

Decluttering isn't a one-time purge — it's a skill you develop over time. Here's a structure to start.

  • 01

    One room at a time

    Don't try to do everything at once. Pick a single room, empty every surface and drawer, and make decisions about each item before moving to the next space.

  • 02

    Three categories only

    Keep, Donate, Discard. Don't create a "maybe" pile — it's just a different form of avoidance. Force the decision now. If you hesitate for more than five seconds, it's probably a donate.

  • 03

    A home for everything kept

    Every item you keep needs a designated place. If you can't assign it a specific home in your space, you're keeping too many things. Disorder is just too much stuff for the space you have.

The Outer Mirrors the Inner

A simpler home is just the beginning. The same clarity you create in your space, you can create in your finances.
